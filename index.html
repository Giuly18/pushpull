Reading the RIR numbers

RIR = reps in reserve — how many more reps you could have done when you racked the weight. 2 RIR means you stopped with two clean reps left in the tank. 0 RIR means you went to the point where another rep wasn't happening with good form.

Heavy compounds sit at 2 RIR: going to failure on overhead press or leg press costs a lot of recovery and adds injury risk for little extra growth. Isolation work — lateral raises, rear delt flyes, curls, calves — can safely run at 0–1 RIR, and that's where you should feel like you emptied the tank.

If you're consistently hitting the top of the rep range at your target RIR, add weight. If you're missing the bottom of the range, the weight's too heavy.

Progression

Double progression: stay at the same weight until you hit the top of the rep range on every set at target RIR, then add weight and start back at the bottom. Leg press might jump 10–20 lb; lateral raises will be 2.5–5 lb, and that's normal.

Deload every 6–8 weeks: one week at roughly 60% of your usual weights. Joints and connective tissue recover slower than muscle.

Do the hip flexor stretch daily, not just on gym days. Highest-leverage thing here for the tilt — twice a week won't outpace sitting the rest of the time.
